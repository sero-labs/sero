Agent and workspace UX — deliverable flows

The audit produced 310 frames of evidence and 10 proposal states. This document sits between them. It groups every captured frame into a flow a person actually walks, states what that flow must keep, and says which flows the current proposals answer. It is the coherence check that has to pass before anything is drawn as an interactive preview.

It exists because the proposals had a real defect, not a presentation problem: two working controls disappeared from them. Redrawing a screen from memory silently deletes what was not remembered.

How a flow was derivedmethod · 5 rules
  • Every captured frame carries a task field: the thing the person was trying to do. There are 76 distinct tasks across the four areas.
  • Each task is assigned to exactly one flow. The assignment is a judgement; the frame counts are not. A script fails if a task lands in two flows or in none, so all 310 frames are accounted for.
  • A second walk records every control by accessible name instead of taking pictures, so the keep lists are the product's own list of 329 controls across 279 screens, not my reading of the frames.
  • A flow ships on its own. It names its screens, its defects, its keep list and its acceptance criteria.
  • Enhance, not replace. A proposal is only an enhancement if every control on the screens it touches is still reachable. Anything removed is named and argued for, not dropped.

Two questions, and nothing below this panel has to be read to answer them. Every flow further down opens to its evidence, its keep list and its acceptance criteria; none of that is needed to say whether the groups and the order are right.

The finding: the unanswered flows are the ones made of controls2 paragraphs

Flows D, E, F and G hold 171 of 310 frames, 55 percent of the evidence, and between them draw one partial proposal state. The three flows that are fully answered are the three made of lists and reading surfaces, which are the easy things to redraw. The unanswered ones are the flows made of controls: changing a cap, picking a model, choosing context, reading history, creating something, moving between apps.

So the instinct that functionality was reduced is right, and it is measurable. The proposals were not a smaller product on purpose. They stopped where drawing got harder, and the stopping point was not written down.

What a screenshot could not tell me: the control walk, and the flow it corrected3 paragraphs

The frames were walked a second time by a pass that records controls by accessible name instead of pixels. It found 329 distinct controls across 279 screens, and 60 of them are named in neither the proposals nor the first draft of the keep lists below. They are not obscure: Back to Rooms and Open in Agent Board are on 103 screens each, Delete Room on 86, Reflect all and More actions on 62, Open in Orchestrator on 57.

This is the argument for the method rather than for my attention. I wrote the first keep lists by reading frames, and they missed a fifth of the product. The lists below now come from the walk.

It also corrects one flow outright. G, Follow the thread, is not the thin flow I called it. By frames it is the smallest, 12 of 310. By controls it is nearly everywhere: Back to Rooms, Back to projects, Open in Agent Board, Open in Orchestrator, Open Room, Open session and Open preview together appear on almost every screen in the product. Counting frames measured how often I photographed navigation, not how much of the product it is.

The seven flows

Each one names what it serves, what it rests on, what is wrong, what must survive the change, and when it is done. The keep list is the contract: those controls exist in the shipped product today and are visible in the evidence.

Rests on

Rooms list (8), Orchestrator Workflow list (5), Orchestrator Home (4), Architect Projects list (2), Workflows tab (2), Workspace tree (2), and two rows reached from a list.

What is wrong

The pill column holds either the phase (BUILD, MAINTAIN) or the overlay (BLOCKED, LIMITED, PAUSED), never both, so a blocked project hides which phase it is in. The header promises "state · phase · spend · needs you".

Three permanent explanatory cards take roughly a third of the first viewport on every visit. Below them each row gives the workflow's title one truncated line, "Release the completed reading trac…", and gives the rest to the agent's instruction.

Fourteen workspaces are listed flat, with no indication of which hold running work, which have Architect projects, or which were opened most recently.

Must survive

  • The tab bar: Home, Workflows, Rooms, Goals, Catalog, Library, with its counts.
  • Reflect all and More actions on the Workflows tab, and dismiss on the Rooms list.
  • Every create entry point: New project, New workflow, the Workflow, Room and Goal cards on Home, and Add workspace.
  • Review on each item in Home's Needs you list.
  • The workspace tree's active-workspace marker and its per-workspace context menu.
  • Found by the control walk, missing from my first list: Search workflows…, Search the library…, Search the catalog…, Clear filters, My Library.

The change

  • One row carries both phase and overlay. They are different facts and neither replaces the other.
  • The row's line belongs to the title. The agent instruction moves behind a disclosure on the same row.
  • The three explanatory cards collapse to one line that can be dismissed, and work moves to the top.
  • A workspace row states whether anything is running in it.

Done when

  • A blocked project shows its phase without being opened.
  • The first viewport of Orchestrator Home is work, not explanation.
  • No list row's primary line is an agent instruction.
  • Every control in the keep list is still one click from the list.

What is wrong

A paused owner and a paused Room read as the same state. The page says work is on hold; it does not say that two Room members have been waiting on the user for over a week, which is the thing that would make someone act.

With prefers-reduced-motion set, nothing is lost, which is the point: the current activity signalling does not depend on animation, but it also does not add a text label where colour is doing the work.

Architect off and Architect on read the same on the project list. With the runtime off, FroggerNeon's row said "Working on M2" at 15:07 for a Workflow that had not reported for three days; the project was marked blocked when Architect next ran. A saved milestone status of "running" outlives its Workflow.

Must survive

  • The existing colour coding. This flow adds a word, it does not take the colour away.
  • The live activity indicator for people who can see motion.

The change

  • Seven named states, each a word before it is a colour: working, queued, waiting for you, paused, complete, failed, unconfirmed.
  • Paused splits by who is paused and whether workers continue underneath.
  • Loading, unavailable and failed each render differently and each say which they are. The empty case is already handled: see the Library in flow F.
  • No percentage and no ETA. A step count is a step count.

Done when

  • Every state is legible with motion disabled and with colour ignored.
  • A paused owner with running workers reads differently from a fully stopped project.
  • No screen claims progress the system has not measured.

Rests on

The largest flow. Six Room result and member views, four Orchestrator workflow step views, the Architect Run inspector, and the milestone and research sections of nine project pages.

What is wrong

Seen on 110 frames. The step card prints the whole agent instruction, often 150 words or more, above the result. The EXPECTED and OUTCOME rows underneath are the clearest thing on the card and are worth keeping. They are simply last.

The MANDATE tab opens on a WORKING INSTRUCTIONS field, a 130-word agent instruction printed in full, above the facts a person came for: model, tools, access level and cost. Those facts are well presented and labelled.

One sentence, "FOV raycasting + fog of war stopped before it finished. Use Retry step here to continue from the failed step.", appears four times on this screen.

The paused Room's summary line is a paragraph addressed to an agent: "APPROVED ARCHITECT SCOPE. This brief and milestone govern the task. Earlier research artifacts are recommendations, not approvals…".

The Run inspector is the worst screen in the audit, and it needs its own piece of work. Its landing state reports nothing: COST, ACTIVE, WAITING, COUNTERS and TOKENS all read "—", the range says "no timings observed", and two filter controls sit above data that has not loaded, over a Load activity button that must be pressed before any of it means anything. Press it on a view with no journal and the product states its own ambiguity: "A view that has not started and one whose telemetry failed look the same here." That is the clearest writing on the screen.

Loaded, it is worse. Nine frames now show it, on the one project in the profile carrying a run journal: every row in the list carries the same 36-character label, owner:01a0ac5a-f8ec-73f5-a728-413dac746997. The column is narrower than the id, so the wrapped text renders on top of the row beneath it and several rows collide in the same pixels. The filter checkboxes are raw ids. MODEL and THINKING both read "not recorded" in the one panel that exists to break spend down by model, TOKENS is 0 with "input, output, cacheRead, cacheWrite unavailable", and two totals sit side by side, $4.6026 and "$4.4835 without call detail", with nothing explaining the difference.

It also arrives pre-filtered without saying so. One owner checkbox is already ticked: "34 of 100 rows, filtered view: $0.0400 of $4.6026". The headline COST reads $4.6026 while every panel below it accounts for $0.0400. The two agree once the filter is noticed, and nothing leads the eye to it.

Tick failures only and the answer is "0 of 100 rows" in small text at the top, over roughly 500 px of empty box containing no message at all. The one sentence rendered in the body is about the chart. A person filtering for failures is told nothing about failures.

Compare the intended design, architect-run-observability, whose rows read "Owner wake · intake", "Research · 3 questions in parallel", "Waiting for your approval of the charter", "Session compacted · 41k tokens summarised" and "diff · 3f1c2ab..9d2e401". The shipped screen prints one opaque id where the prototype names what happened. This is a rethink, not a tidy-up. It is tracked as its own piece of work in issue #535, which starts from the prototype rather than from the current screen. The screen stays in this flow so it is not lost between the two.

Must survive

  • The full agent instruction, behind a disclosure. It is never truncated and never summarised away.
  • The EXPECTED and OUTCOME rows, which the audit found worth keeping as they are.
  • Model, tools, access level and cost on the member mandate.
  • The plan map, named in the evidence as the clearest thing in the Orchestrator.
  • Retry step, and every other recovery action on a failed step.
  • The Run inspector as a distinct surface, with its raw record intact: the View run picker, the per-activity filters, failures only, Clear filters, Zoom in / Zoom out, the cumulative spend chart, and the by-activity and by-model breakdowns. All of it survives a rethink of how the rows are labelled.
  • Found by the control walk, missing from my first list: Evidence at <sha> on 56 screens, which links a claim to the commit that proves it; Zoom in and Zoom out on the plan map; Open preview; Attempt history.

The change

  • Order inverts. Outcome, then evidence, then the instruction that produced it.
  • A sentence appears once per screen, at the level that owns it.
  • Long agent prose gets headings, numbered steps, labelled evidence and acceptance criteria. It is folded, not cut.
  • An agent-addressed paragraph is not used as a human-facing summary line.

Done when

  • The conclusion is above the fold on every step, member and milestone view.
  • No sentence is repeated on one screen.
  • Every word of the original instruction is still reachable in two clicks or fewer.
  • Nothing is truncated to make it fit.

How the previews should be drawn

The question behind this whole document: rebuild each screen as components, or work from the captured frames? Here is the recommendation and the reason.

Draw the screen in the product's style, from real records. This was a choice between drawn screens and overlays on the captured frames. You chose drawn screens, in the style of proposals.html. Every name, count, date and amount comes from the project, Workflow and Room records, not from memory.

A drawn screen can silently lose a control, which is how the first proposals lost two. The control list below each screen is what stops that.

Every preview state carries a control parity list naming each control the shipped screen has, from the control walk, as kept, moved, folded behind a disclosure, or dropped with a reason. A new control is listed as added. A drop or an addition waits for your approval. A state with an unlabelled control is not finished.

Notes and limits

Carried out of this document as its own task

The Run inspector needs a rethink of its own. It is the one screen in this audit where the shipped result and the intended prototype are not the same idea. Its nine frames count in flow C, because they answer flow C's question, and its keep list holds every control it ships with today. The redesign is issue #535, with architect-run-observability as the starting point.

What this document does not establish4 limits

The keep lists now come from the control walk, so they hold every control the walk reached. They do not hold a control on a screen the walk never opened, and the 18 gaps are exactly those screens.

The loaded Run inspector is no longer one of them. It was, on the first pass: the run journal is served by the Architect runtime, which stayed off, so the loaded state photographed as empty.
